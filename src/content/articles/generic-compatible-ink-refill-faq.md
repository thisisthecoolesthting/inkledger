Generic ink, compatible cartridges, and refill kits are where most of the real savings live in printer ownership -- but they're also where the most misinformation circulates, especially around whether they'll damage your printer or void your warranty. The FAQ below covers what's actually true, sourced from how these systems mechanically work rather than manufacturer marketing.

## Where the real savings are

The short version: compatible cartridges and refill kits, such as this [AYMSous universal dye ink refill kit](/products/b09q8mvcrg-aymsous-6-pack-universal-dye-ink-refill-kit-for-all-hp-canon/), are legal to use under U.S. warranty law and mechanically compatible with the vast majority of inkjet printers. The one real restriction to watch for is HP's Instant Ink / HP+ program, which locks a printer to genuine HP cartridges as a condition of subscription enrollment -- that's the source of almost every "my printer won't accept generic ink" complaint, not a universal anti-generic-ink defense built into all printers.

## The bottom line

If you're not enrolled in a cartridge subscription program, generic ink and refill kits work in your printer exactly the way OEM cartridges do, with the tradeoff concentrated in photo-print color accuracy rather than everyday text and document printing.
